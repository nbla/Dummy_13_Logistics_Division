# Dummy13 Logistics Division

*A completely reasonable logistics organization operated by small plastic robots.*

Welcome to the archive of **Dummy13 Logistics Division**.

This repository documents a growing collection of 3D-printed Dummy13 units, RC vehicles, expedition vehicles, questionable engineering projects and the increasingly elaborate fictional organization that somehow emerged around them.

None of this was planned.

That is probably important.

---

## How This Started

Logistics Division did not begin with a worldbuilding document, a carefully designed organization or even particularly ambitious plans.

The Operator simply got increasingly interested in 3D printing, building things and making small machines again.

One project led to another.

Dummy13 figures were printed.

RC vehicles appeared.

Parts were modified.

Accessories were designed.

Things broke.

Other things were repaired.

Occasionally the repairs became upgrades.

The printed figures started appearing around the projects.

Then they started acquiring names.

Then jobs.

Then personalities.

Eventually they started acquiring firmware histories, vehicle assignments, documented operational procedures and opinions regarding one another.

At some point during this process, **Dummy13 Logistics Division** happened.

Nobody recorded exactly when.

---

## The Division

Logistics Division is best described as a fictional logistics, engineering and expedition organization built around real physical projects.

The vehicles actually exist.

The robots actually exist.

Most of the modifications, repairs, experiments and strange situations documented here are based on things that genuinely happened while building, driving, printing or photographing them.

The organizational structure surrounding those events is considerably less real.

Probably.

The Division currently operates several categories of assets, including:

- Dummy13 personnel
- Division Vehicles
- Expedition Vehicles
- Experimental equipment
- An unreasonable quantity of spare parts

Other branches and projects may appear as the organization continues to expand.

Expansion has historically required very little justification.

---

## Dummy13 Crew

The heart of Logistics Division is its collection of 3D-printed Dummy13 units.

They exist at several scales and have gradually developed individual roles, personalities and histories.

Some are competent professionals.

Some are competent professionals with concerning methods.

Some are Glitch.

The crew currently includes command personnel, mechanics, technicians, navigators, field operators, expedition specialists and units whose exact purpose remains unclear.

Their personalities were not designed all at once.

Most developed organically through photographs, projects, accidents, recurring jokes and the simple realization that a particular plastic robot clearly *would* behave a certain way.

This has resulted in an organization where firmware versions are documented seriously, interpersonal relationships are documented less seriously, and a blind unit named Dust has:

**Vision**

listed under Weaknesses.

The documentation department stands by this assessment.

## Division Vehicles

The **DV series** contains the larger and more conventional Logistics Division ground vehicles.

Some are commercial RC platforms.

Some are heavily modified.

Some are largely 3D printed.

Some are not yet built despite already having parts, crew assignments or operational plans.

Current vehicles range from compact utility trucks to full-size 1:10 trail vehicles and large ongoing construction projects.

Their records document both technical configuration and operational history.

This means a vehicle profile may contain useful information about tires, electronics and suspension immediately followed by an assessment such as:

**Weakness: Shortcut has access to maps.**

Both forms of information are considered operationally relevant.

---

## Expedition Vehicles

The **EV series** consists primarily of small SCX30-based expedition crawlers.

These vehicles combine micro-scale RC platforms with printed bodies, scale accessories and varying degrees of good judgment.

EV01 began as a green JLU.

Then it became a Nissan Patrol.

Then it became a Wagoneer.

More Wagoneers followed.

At least one body has been deliberately exposed to open flame.

EV02 is pink and carries a canoe.

EV03 is a white office-based Jimny whose planned higher-resolution replacement was defeated by repeated printer failures.

EV04 remains comparatively normal.

This is being monitored.

## How Lore Works

The lore in this repository is intentionally allowed to grow from the physical hobby rather than dictate it.

A vehicle may develop a history because something actually broke during a drive.

A character may develop a personality because they repeatedly appeared in photographs doing something ridiculous.

A modification may become part of the fictional organization because it genuinely worked.

Sometimes a joke becomes recurring behavior.

Sometimes recurring behavior becomes canon.

This is how Glitch became Glitch.

There is no requirement that every unit immediately have a complete biography, firmware history or specialist role.

Unknown information is allowed to remain unknown.

The archive grows when the projects do.

## A Note About Glitch

If you arrived here after seeing Logistics Division posts elsewhere, there is a reasonable chance you already know Glitch.

Glitch is a Driver, Courier and Autonomous Field Operator.

This is the official description.

A less official description would be:

Glitch tends to complete tasks before anyone has finished deciding whether he should do them.

His official motto is:

**"Already done."**

His unofficial incident-reporting protocol begins:

**Glitch:** "Good news."

**Node:** "No."

If this exchange occurs, the operation has probably already been completed.

Documentation will follow.

## The Operator

Behind Logistics Division is the Operator.

The Operator prints the parts, builds the vehicles, drives them, repairs them, photographs them and occasionally makes decisions that later require explanation in the archive.

The Operator is not formally listed as Logistics Division personnel.

This arrangement provides useful administrative distance.

The Division grew largely because the Operator rediscovered how much fun it is to make things.

3D printing led to figures.

Figures led to vehicles.

Vehicles led to modifications.

Modifications led to increasingly unnecessary projects.

Somewhere along the way, a collection of hobby projects turned into a small shared universe with its own history.

The Operator continues to deny that this was the plan.

Available evidence supports this claim.

## The Archive

This repository is the long-term archive for Dummy13 Logistics Division.

The working master is maintained in Obsidian and periodically committed here when records have been reviewed and cleaned up.

The purpose is partly practical:

- Keep track of vehicle configurations
- Remember which tires are actually installed on which crawler
- Preserve character and firmware lore
- Record modifications and project history
- Prevent useful information from disappearing into old conversations
- Maintain version history as the Division evolves

It is also here because documenting all of this far beyond any reasonable requirement is funny.

Both objectives are considered equally valid.

## Canon and Version History

The current records represent the best available Logistics Division canon.

They are expected to change.

New projects happen.

Old photographs are rediscovered.

Forgotten details return.

Vehicles receive modifications.

Characters develop.

Occasionally somebody remembers that the tire documented on one vehicle is actually installed on another.

Git exists for a reason.

Older versions remain part of the archive history, but the current files should be treated as the active record.

## Final Assessment

Dummy13 Logistics Division began because making things was fun.

It continued because making increasingly unnecessary things was even more fun.

What started as 3D printing and RC projects gradually accumulated names, personalities, vehicles, history and enough internal documentation to require version control.

There is no roadmap for where it goes next.

That would imply planning.

For now, the operating procedure remains:

1. Build something
2. See what happens
3. Give it a name
4. Document the consequences
